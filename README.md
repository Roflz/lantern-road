# Lantern Road

A dark-fantasy idle party battler, built for a phone on its side: every screen is landscape. Six lantern-bearers fight on their own, by the rules you give them. The design lives in [docs/design.md](docs/design.md).

## The idle battle prototype (build steps 1–7)

Open `index.html` in a browser. It needs no build step and no internet connection; the fonts ship in `fonts/`. A new game starts with one hero, picked from the Sellsword, the Hexblade and the Pilgrim, and 100 gold. The camp brings the rest of the party, and they walk the whole Lantern Road: 88 stages through five regions, each with two elites and a boss.

- **The five regions:** the Gravefields (levels 1–10, the Sexton), the Drowned Chapel (11–30, the Drowned Bishop), Thornwood (31–50, the Antlered One), the Ashen Keep (51–75, the Burnt Marshal) and the Hollow Throne (76–100, the Hollow King). Each has its own foes, battle backdrop, weather, map and material, and each boss's first clear grants its Relic and opens the next region, with its buildings and the classes that join the Recruit Board. The first time the party walks into a region, its name comes up before the stage's.

- **The Hearth:** the camp, the main screen, as views around one fire (screen 2; the design is `docs/mockups/camp-views.html`). Drag sideways (or press ← and →) to turn the camera; let go and it eases to the nearer view, at once with Reduced motion. The **party view** is close on the fire: the party sits round it in their gear, the Recruit Board stands among them on the left, Wick, the lamp-keeper, keeps to the bottom-left corner, and the amber road zigzags away behind the fire to the boss's chapel, showing the frontier region's stages: lit lanterns glow, elites are red diamonds and the boss a skull, the frontier lantern gutters, and past it the road is dark. The **buildings view** is turned 180°, lower and further back: the plots open from the middle outwards, built ones show their building, empty ones the plot, and plots Renown hasn't cleared are gaps; the camp's wall, the graveyard, the crypt and will-o'-wisps lie beyond. The camera stays with the party until the first blueprint comes. Over the scene: the lead hero's face, your name (Traveller until Wick's welcome brings one) and Renown (tap for its track); the next goal, with Go (a free recruit, points to spend, a blueprint to build, buildings to collect from, or the frontier stage to clear); gold and bone dust, each with + to where you earn more; ? (help) and ⚙ (Settings); the formation as it stands in battle, at the right under gold and bone dust, with level and fatigue (tap a face for the hero, drag to swap places, tap an empty slot to add someone); and the ribbon of Heroes, Tactics and Bag beside the corner's big button, Set out (up the road to the frontier, its card open) in the party view and Collect (every building's store, as Gather it all does) in the buildings view. Plaques name what stands in the scene and amber chips show what's waiting. Tap the road to fly up it, a building to open it, an empty plot to build, the board to recruit, a hero or Wick for a word, or the fire for its free Healing Draughts. The Hearth has no tab bar; the other screens' Hearth tab comes back to it.
- **The road view:** the same scene, up the road (the design is `docs/mockups/road-view.html`). Tap the road at the Hearth, or Set out, and the camera flies from the fire up the road to the frontier, its card open: one more tap sets out. Drag up or down to look along the road (the wheel, or ↑ and ↓, step a stage); every stage is the same drag away, and the view settles on the nearest, a flick carrying it further. Lanterns stand on posts, lit to your light's edge, where the party waits; past it the foes stand by the road as silhouettes, the boss's chapel stands at the end and the gate to the next region beyond it. Labels show each stage's id, the frontier's name, the boss's name and bubbles over stages whose first clear brings something new. The card shows the stage's id, name, level and party size (green when you have them), its foes and what it gives (a first clear's unlocks marked); tap it for the stage in full (the stage sheet). Its big button is Set out at the frontier, Farm on a lit stage, Locked beyond your light. The region's name sits at the top with ‹ › to the regions before (the next stays shut until its gate opens). Drag back past the first stage, tap ‹, or press Escape, and the camera flies home.
- **Buildings:** 3 plots to start, up to 14. Fifteen buildings, each with five levels: the Recruit Board (outside the plots), Toll House, Infirmary, Training Yard, Shop, Tactician, Scavenger Camp, Barracks, Salvage Pit, Masters' Hall, Shrine, and the passive Whetstone, Ossuary, Hexing Stone and Bell Tower. Region I's first clears turn them into blueprints. Building is instant; levels 1–2 cost gold and Bone Dust, and level 3 and up the building's home material. Levels 3 and 5 offer a choice of two, which can be switched later. Tearing a building down frees its plot and gives nothing back; what it taught stays.
- **Renown:** the account level. Stages lit, battle XP, bosses, level thresholds, new classes, and buildings built or upgraded raise it, and it clears plots 4 to 14. Tapping your panel at the Hearth's top left opens its track.
- **The Recruit Board:** it opens at 1-2, and its first recruit is free. Each board shows recruits with rolled stats (bars show where each roll falls in its class's range), priced by level and quality: one hire per board, and a new board when the party comes home or every 4 hours. Its three tracks add recruits, picks and higher levels, up to storied recruits who arrive combined or awakened. The Barracks raises the roster cap from 8. Swipe a hero left on the Party tab to dismiss them: only their gear comes back.
- **Fatigue and rest:** each turn a hero takes adds 1 fatigue, changed by Iron Will, Comfort, Laudanum, Peaceful Rest, Cold Compress, Transfusion, House Calls and gear. Weary (50) costs 5% of Power, Defense and Speed, Exhausted (75) 15%, and Spent (100) keeps a hero home until they fall below 50. Heroes rest at the fire (10 an hour), in the Infirmary's beds (20–35), in the Training Yard or at a lesson, whether the app is open or closed.
- **Idle buildings and time away:** the Toll House (gold, or party items), the Scavenger Camp (a region's material, and rare finds), the Training Yard (XP, up to the level of your highest lit stage) and the Salvage Pit (gear rolls) produce on real time, up to their storage. Coming back after 5 minutes or more, "While you were away" lists what came in, who rested, lessons finished and new faces, with Gather it all. Closing the app never plays battles: only buildings work while you're gone.
- **The economy:** the loadout draws party items from the Bag, so nothing refills for free. The Shop sells party items (dearer as the road goes on) and a small gear stall; a free Healing Draught waits by the fire every 4 hours, up to 3; the Infirmary's still-room brews more. Gear sells for 10, 25 or 60 gold × S.
- **Training:** the Masters' Hall teaches rule slots IV–VI and skill slots I–II while the hero is away (a combined hero trains up to the highest rule-slot maximum among their classes). The Shrine respecs for gold. The Tactician builds on Tactician I (the 1-11 clear): a 3rd party rule slot at level 1, then loadout slots 3 and 4, more party rule slots, and Lessons II and III.

- **The title:** every boot opens on it. The party walks the Lantern Road under a full moon while the game loads (the fonts, the sprites, the save and its cloud copy), and the road's ten lanterns light, near to far, as the loading goes: at least 1.5 seconds, so they have time. Then the horizon glows and Tap to begin pulses. One tap anywhere starts the sound and opens the game: the camp (with "While you were away" first after 5 minutes or more), or a new save's first pick. The build tag sits in the corner (`BUILD` in `js/title.js`).
- **Servers** (a placeholder for multiplayer): the pill above Tap to begin opens the Servers. Your servers show your lead hero and your Renown there; the newest, one past the highest you've used, is New. Each server keeps its own save: S1 keeps the save the game always had (the same key, and `data/users/<id>/game` in the cloud), and S2 and up save beside it (`-s2`, `game-s2`). A server you have a save on switches at once; a new one asks first, then starts a fresh save there. The title stays up, and the next tap begins on that server. The last server used opens next time.
- **The first hour:** a new game opens on the title, then the first pick. One system at a time, as first clears unlock it (campaign spec §8): a one-line pointer the first time something matters (the turn flags and speed buttons in the first battle, the rule each flag names, the draughts, the Tactics screen's rules, the free recruit), and the results popup shows everything a trip unlocked as New, each going straight to it (Open Tactics, Recruit, The loadout, Build it, See the Relic, The map). Dots on the tab bar (and on the Hearth's Heroes and Bag buttons) show where something waits. After a loss Old Wick gives the stage's hint, and at the frontier the popup offers to farm the stage before it. Settings → First-time tips turns the pointers and tips off. Nothing appears before it's useful: the trip settings wait for Keep going (1-6), and rule sets for Tactician I.
- **The Road tab:** the region map, read from the bottom up. Lit stages are burning lanterns whose light joins up; the frontier lantern gutters with the party beside it; the dark stages above show their foes only as silhouettes. Elites are red diamonds and the boss a skull. Tap the region's name for the whole road, all five regions; a region stays dark until the boss before it falls.
- **The stage sheet:** tap a lantern. The stage's foes in their formation (tap one for its stats, skills, phases and resistances), the recommended level and party size coloured by how ready you are, its rewards and first-clear unlocks, the trip settings, the party with their fatigue (and, for Spent heroes, when they're fit again, which opens the Infirmary's beds), and Set out (the frontier) or Farm (a lit stage). The trip settings' "Come home when" chips switch the between-battle party rules: fatigue 75+, an ally fell, before a boss or elite, and out of Healing Draughts. A chip needs a free party rule slot.
- **Trips:** the party sets out, fights, and with "Keep going after a win" (from 1-6) walks on to the next stage, until a battle is lost, a between-battle party rule calls it home (on by default: any hero at 75+ fatigue), the boss falls, every hero is Spent, or you call it home. A Spent hero walks home alone while the rest carry on. Closing the app brings the party home after the battle under way, which is resolved on your return; nothing plays while the app is closed. The results popup shows what a trip brought home; a trip that ended while the app was closed shows its summary on the Road tab.
- **Rewards:** every foe defeated drops gold, XP and a chance of loot by its rank; a win adds the clear reward by the stage's tier, doubled for gold and materials on a first clear; Bone Dust; party items and gear into the Bag (a full Bag sells new gear where it drops). XP is a share of a level at the stage's level, so a hero below it catches up fast. Called foes drop nothing.
- **First clears teach:** the Tactics screen (1-1), party items (1-3), new conditions in every region but the last, Keep going (1-6), Tactician I (1-11: thresholds, a dozen conditions, the Move action, Focus fire and saved rule sets) and each boss's Relic, and they bring the buildings' blueprints. The Tactician's Lesson II (its level 3) brings the Gather action.
- **Foe behaviour:** melee foes placed behind wait and step up when a front slot opens; summoners call foes into empty slots; bosses change at a share of their health, with a callout; wandering foes (a Moth Swarm, a Cage Mimic that runs at round 6) can join replays. From Region II: healers and raisers, Silence, turn delays, bursting corpses, mana drain (the Font Leech), wolf packs, foes that start Hidden (the Hanged Man), Lure and Reorder the Court pulling a back-row hero forward (Move resistance resists, and the Move action walks back), thorns that hurt attackers (Mother Bramble), charged blasts on a whole row, regeneration (the Antlered One), covers, siege shots, splashing blows and a burning aura (the Burnt Marshal), stolen and snuffed buffs, confusion, and the Hollow King's three phases.
- **The Party tab:** Arrange moves heroes between the six slots, and leaves some out, so a party of 1 to 6 sets out. Each hero shows their fatigue and where they are: in the party, at the fire, in a bed, training, at a lesson, or Spent and when they'll be fit again.

- **Landscape:** every screen is landscape (the decision of 2026-11-04). The game is laid out in a frame 390 pixels tall and 694–936 wide, to suit the window's shape, then scaled to fit. At 844×390, a phone on its side, it fills the screen; other windows letterbox. A touch phone held upright gets the frame drawn on its side, so with rotation lock on you simply turn the phone; a window with a mouse is never turned. The frame keeps clear of the notches. Battles, the results popup and the report fill it. The other screens are redesigned for landscape one at a time: the title and the Hearth so far. Until then each keeps its portrait layout, tab bar included, in a centred column.
- **Heroes:** each has rolled stats, a basic attack, their class's starter skill and a short list of rules. Front-row classes take the front slots, nearest the foes.
- **Characters grow:** battles give XP, up to level 200. Each level brings a stat point, and every fifth a skill point. Thresholds at 30, 50, 80, 100, 120, 150, 180 and 200 open the next tier of a skill tree, combine with another class (at 50 and 200) or open an awakening (from 100), and each brings a free respec. Levels roll over on the results popup's XP bars after the battle.
- **The hero sheet:** tap a hero on the Party screen. Stats shows the hero on a small stage where skills play on a training dummy, the points to spend and the equipped skills. Skills holds the skill tree: 28 skills per class in three build paths, each with levels 1–5 and an awakened form. Rules opens the rules editor.
- **Gear:** every hero carries a Common weapon of their class's type, and can wear armour and an accessory. The content spec's 92 items are all in: 42 weapons, 15 armour, 30 accessories (12 of them class-only) and 5 Relics. Most trade a bonus for a cost, or work under a condition. Each piece has a rarity (Common, Fine, Rare or Relic) and an item level, and its plain stats grow with that level. Every piece changes how the hero is drawn, in battle, on the hero sheet and in menus.
- **The Gear tab and the Bag:** on the hero sheet, tap a piece in the Bag to try it on the sprite, and again for its card: what it gives always, what it costs and when it works, against what the hero wears now. The Bag tab holds up to 60 unworn pieces and sells the ones you don't want; when it's full, new gear sells where it drops. Stages drop loot now; the Bag's Prototype panel still rolls gear at any region's rates.
- **Rules:** on each turn a character reads its rules from the top and uses the first one that is true and possible. Only equipped skills fire. If no rule applies, it uses its basic attack.
- **Turn flags:** banners along the top show who acts next and the skill each will use. The first is the turn being played, with the rule that chose it.
- **The back row:** melee attacks must hit the front row while anyone stands there. Skills with reach leap past it, and a Guard pulls single-target attacks onto the tank.
- **Statuses and resistances:** the content spec's statuses. A harmful one lands on a roll of its chance minus the target's resistance (Stun, Move, Bleed, Blight, Curse, Debuff).
- **Mana and enrage:** skills cost mana, and basic attacks restore some. From round 15 the foes are enraged and hit harder every round, so no battle can stall.
- **Watching:** battles play on their own, at 1×, 2× or 4×. Pause, then tap Turn to play one turn at a time, or skip to the result. Between battles on a trip the party walks on by itself; the card that shows the rewards has Report and Come home.
- **The results:** when a battle brings the party home, a popup over the dimmed battlefield (`docs/mockups/results.html`): Victory or Driven back, the stage and First clear; each hero's XP bar filling from where they were and rolling over at each level, and their fatigue bar with its Weary and Exhausted marks (tap it for what that means); the loot popping in (tap gear for its card) and anything New. Then Report, Hearth, Fight again and Next (the next stage's sheet on the Road), or after a loss Try again and, at a wall, Farm the stage before it, while Old Wick gives the stage's hint. It fills in about 4 seconds; a tap skips to the end. After a trip of several battles it shows the trip's totals.
- **The report:** opened from the results, it scrolls as one: what the battle brought home and why the trip ended; damage dealt and taken, and healing done and received, for every character. Tap a character to see how often each of its rules fired and its best moments.
- **Formation:** a tap on the Hearth's formation grid opens it (`docs/mockups/formation.html`): the party standing as in battle, the back two columns and the front on the right, facing the next stage's foes, with the roster along the bottom. Drag a portrait onto a place, a hero onto another place to swap them, or a hero down to the roster to rest them (one always stays); tap a hero for their sheet. A melee hero in the back shows Defends only, a Spent hero is greyed (but can be placed), and the Oathbound Gorget keeps its wearer in front. Auto fills the open places with rested heroes, front-row classes in front, the highest level first.
- **Prototype tools,** for play-testing without the grind, in one panel: Settings → Prototype tools (or the camp's Prototype chip). Light the road stage by stage or up to a region (with its first-clear unlocks and Renown), add gold and materials, train everyone (+1, +5 or +25 levels, or to the road's level), spend every point, rest everyone, fill the party with free recruits, every blueprint, Renown, skip 1 or 8 hours, roll gear at any region's rates, learn every condition, and show the first-time tips again. Its "Show prototype tools" switch also hides the buttons in place (the camp's chip, Train on the hero sheet, Roll gear in the Bag and on the Gear tab, Learn all on Tactics). They are not part of the game: `js/proto.js` says how to remove them.
- **Sound:** sound effects only; the music is off until it is reworked.
- **Keys:** at the Hearth, ← and → turn between the views. In battle, Space or P plays and pauses, N plays one turn, F changes the speed, S skips, and Esc opens the menu.

### Tests

- `node tools/simtest.js [battles per stage]` simulates hundreds of battles per stage and checks every event log for broken rules. It tests the home base: a new game, the Recruit Board, the gold over Region I's first clears against the campaign's party sizes, rest and beds, each battle's fatigue, idle buildings and time away (the same after a reload), costs, choices and tearing down, Renown, the Hall, the Shrine, the Tactician, the Shop, dismissing and the save. It ends with the campaign: the five regions' data and art, each region's engine pieces, rewards, fatigue, unlocks and trips, and the reference-party report (campaign spec §5.6), every stage of every region one and two levels either side of its recommendation against its target, with each region's farming loop. `node tools/simtest.js --ref [battles] [--regions=2,3]` runs only that report (`tools/reference.js`). It also runs the party at several levels, rules and skills experiments, a fuzz of random builds across all twelve classes, and tests of the hero model, the save migration, the servers (each keeps its own save, and switching leaves the others intact) and the rules model. For gear, it checks that every item's effect fires, runs random gear on random builds, compares a Common, Fine and Rare weapon in the same fights, and tests loot odds, the Bag and equipping.
- `node tools/playthrough.js [runs per starting hero] [--to=regions]` plays a new game through Regions I and II (or as far as `--to` says) with the game's own mechanics and no prototype tools, as a sensible player would: the first pick, trips, recruiting, building, points, gear, draughts, rest on a simulated clock, and farming the stage before a wall. It fails on a stall, a softlock or an error, and checks that every unlock, blueprint and first-time tip comes when the campaign spec says. It prints each run's play and rest time, its hires, and a stage-by-stage timeline.
- `python tools/playthrough_ui.py [hero]` does the same in headless Chromium through the game's own buttons and taps (at the Hearth: Set out and the road, each flying up the road to the frontier's card, the Recruit Board, turning to the buildings view and an empty plot; then the road view's Set out, or ↓ and Farm, Skip, the results popup's Report and Hearth, Hire, Build), touching the Hearth's scene where it is drawn, and records every coach mark, tip and new thing as it appears, and every console error.
- `python tools/results_check.py [--shots DIR]` plays five scenes into the results popup (1-1's first clear, a four-hero win, a six-hero win, a loss at 1-9's wall, a trip of three battles) at 844×390, 667×375, 1280×720, 900×800 and 390×844 turned, and checks what it says, that it fits the frame, the guide's `data-tut` hooks (a coach mark can ring them), that a tap skips the fill, and its taps and buttons, tapped where they are drawn (through the turn, when turned), and that reduced motion shows the end at once. The screenshots are in `docs/screens/results/`.
- `python tools/formation_check.py [--sizes ...] [--shots DIR]` opens Formation from the Hearth's grid at 844×390, 667×375, 1280×720 and 390×844 turned, and drags with the mouse or a finger where things are drawn: a portrait onto an open place, a swap, a rest (and the last hero staying), Auto leaving the Spent hero out, Defends only, a Spent hero placed, the Oathbound Gorget's refusal, a long roster scrolling under a drag, a tap for the hero sheet, Done and the ✕; it checks the panel fits the frame and the guide's `data-tut` anchors. The screenshots are in `docs/screens/formation/`.
- `python tools/guide_check.py [--sizes …] [--shots DIR]` walks Wick's first session from a new game through real input, at 844×390 and 390×844 turned by default: each step's line, a press away from the lit thing held back, the name box, the first pick and the free recruit, both battles, the results, the hero's tactics, and the drag in Formation; then the session is done, the goal tracker takes over, and Replay the guide shows his first line again. The screenshots are in `docs/screens/guide/`.
- `python tools/shot.py index.html out.png` takes screenshots in headless Chromium and prints console errors (needs Playwright). `--w` and `--h` set the window, 390×844 by default: a touch phone held upright, so the frame is turned. It taps through the title once it has loaded, as a player would (`--title` stays on it). A steps file can wait for the page (`until:<expression>`), so a screenshot can catch a boss's phase change, and a `size:<w>,<h>` step resizes the window.
- `python tools/frame_check.py [--w W --h H] [--touch]` checks the landscape frame through real input, turned (390×844 with touch, the default), flat or at a desktop size: the title covers the frame and its server picker opens; a tap lands where it was aimed, the camp fills the stage with its scene at 2× and no tab bar, its Set out flies up the road, its camera nudges back before the first blueprint and turns under a drag along the frame (not across it) to settle on the nearer view, taps in its scene find the fire, the road (and fly up it) and the Recruit Board, its formation swaps by drag, a roster row's swipe opens Dismiss, rules reorder by drag, the Party tab, Settings, the Road map and Tactics scroll the way the finger (or the wheel) goes, a battle fills the frame with its art at 3× and its coach mark on target, starting a second server from the title and switching back leaves both saves intact, and the frame keeps clear of emulated notches.
- `python tools/road_check.py [--sizes …] [--shots DIR]` checks the road view through real input at five sizes: Set out flies up the road to the frontier's card, a drag of 64 design pixels moves one stage and a flick carries on, the labels (ids, the frontier's callout, the boss, the bubbles, the gate held past the boss), Locked, Farm and Set out on the cards, a tap on a stage, the wheel, a drag back past 1-1 and ‹ home, a tap on the road at the Hearth, and the card's stage sheet. `--shots docs/screens/road-view` takes its screenshots.
- `python tools/camp_perf.py [--w W --h H] [--rates 1,4]` measures the camp's frame time in headless Chromium: a camp mid Region I and a late one, at rest in each view and while turning, with the CPU at full speed and throttled 4× (a mid-range phone). It prints the scene's drawing time a frame, the frames it draws a second (thirty at rest) and the main thread's time a frame.
- `python tools/cloud_check.py` checks each server's cloud save against a stand-in for the artifact host's db: S1 under `data/users/<id>/game`, S2 under `game-s2`, the index under `servers`, nothing under the old roguelike's `save`, and a second device opening both servers from the cloud.
- `tools/sprites.html` shows every sprite and icon at a large size: all twelve classes from the side and from behind, every weapon, armour and accessory on a hero, the thralls, the item icons and every foe by region. Add `?part=classes`, `weapons`, `armour`, `accessories`, `icons` or `foes` for one part.

## The first version: a roguelike

A dark-fantasy roguelike in a single HTML file, `lantern-road.html`, kept in the repository for now. Your lantern-bearer walks a cursed road on their own. You steer between lanes to choose forks, fight turn-based battles, and try to end the Hollow King.

### Play

Open `lantern-road.html` in a browser. It needs no build step, no server and no internet connection: the fonts ship in `fonts/`.

- **Move between lanes:** arrow keys, A/D, the on-screen arrows, or tap either side of the road.
- **Choose actions:** number keys 1–5, or click.
- **Pause:** P or Esc.
- **Speed:** the speed button switches between 1× and 2×. It speeds up walking, fight animations and auto-play decisions.
- **Auto:** the Auto button makes the game play itself. It picks forks, steers toward oil and gold, fights, and makes event and shop choices. Pressing Pause or opening Relics stops it until you resume.
- **Tips:** tap any icon, enemy intent or status to see what it means. The first walk teaches forks, intents, draughts, oil and relic sets with one-time hints.
- **Settings:** sound effects, music, and screen shake and motion can each be turned off. Settings also holds How to Play and Erase progress.

Speed, auto play, settings and the chosen Darkness level are remembered on the device.

### Developer mode

Tap the **Lantern Road** title seven times, or open the page with `#dev` at the end of the address. Developer mode adds 3×, 5× and 10× speeds; with Auto on at 10×, a full walk takes about a minute and a half. Settings has a button to leave developer mode.

### Rules

- **The run:** 5 regions (Gravefields, Drowned Chapel, Thornwood, Ashen Keep, Hollow Throne), each with 10 events and a boss.
- **Events:** Fight, Elite, Mystery, Campfire, Merchant, Shrine, Treasure.
- **Lantern oil:** burns as you walk. When it runs out, the dark takes 1 HP every few steps.
- **Combat:** turn-based. The foe shows its next move. You can Strike, Guard, use your class skill, drink a draught (a free action), or flee normal fights. Strikes and damage skills show how their damage adds up, relic by relic.
- **Relic sets:** many relics belong to a build style: Blood, Ward, Hex or Lantern. Carrying 3 of one style switches on its set bonus (Bloodletter, Bulwark, Hexed Path, Lamplighter).
- **Scoring:**

  | Action | Points |
  |---|---|
  | Normal enemy | +10 |
  | Elite | +50 |
  | Boss | +200 |
  | Region cleared | +100 |
  | Gold held at the end | +1 per gold |
  | Victory | +500, then the total ×1.5 |
  | Darkness level | the final total ×(1 + 0.25 per level) |

### Progression

- **Embers:** every run earns 1 ember per 100 points. Spend them at the Pyre to unlock classes (Hexblade, Pilgrim), relics, mystery events, and a starting-relic choice.
- **Achievements:** 21 achievements, each worth a few embers. They are listed in the Codex.
- **Codex:** achievements, a bestiary of every foe you have defeated, and every relic you have found.
- **Darkness:** winning unlocks the next Darkness level, up to V. Levels stack: more foe HP, a hungrier lantern, harder elites and bosses, pricier merchants.
- **Daily Road:** one shared road per calendar day, with the same class, foes and forks for every player. One walk per day, double embers, and a streak for playing on consecutive days. It ignores Pyre unlocks so everyone draws from the same pool.

### Look and sound

- **Cozy gothic:** a violet night with warm amber light. The lantern swings as the hero walks, wayside lanterns and grave candles glow along the road, and a soft vignette frames the scene.
- **Icons instead of words:** the HUD, enemy intents, statuses, combat actions, prices and rewards all use pixel icons with numbers.
- **Feedback:** pickups fly into their counters, counters bump, big hits flash and shake the screen, foes lunge before they strike, relics come out of an opening chest, and the end-of-walk score counts up.
- **The Pyre:** a campfire scene. Each unlock lights one of the ten lanterns around it.
- **Music and sound:** each region has its own procedural ambient tune with a drone, a soft plucked melody, lantern crackle, and rain in the Drowned Chapel. All sounds pass through a soft filter and a short echo.

### Saving

- **The walk in progress** is saved before each event and every few seconds while walking. If the game closes, Continue on the title screen picks it up. If it closed during an event, that event starts again.
- **Embers, unlocks, records, achievements and the Daily Road streak:** when the page runs as a Claude artifact, they save to the viewer's account. Opened anywhere else, they save to the browser's local storage.
