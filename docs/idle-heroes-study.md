# Idle Heroes Study

From your 60 screenshots of a new account's first 20 minutes or so in Idle Heroes, taken 2026-11-04 and kept in `Downloads/Telegram Desktop`. Their game runs in landscape, as ours will. This study covers:
- what their tutorial does, step by step
- why their screens read so easily
- what Lantern Road takes from it, screen by screen

Their lines are paraphrased here, not quoted.

## 2. Why it reads so clearly

1. **One action per step.** A glove points at exactly one thing and the rest of the screen dims. You tap, and the game moves on.
2. **Short lines.** Each line is one or two short sentences, warm and plain, with no jargon.
3. **Two ways of speaking.**
   - A wide panel along the bottom for welcomes and praise, with a ▼ to continue.
   - A speech bubble beside her for instructions.
4. **The guide never covers the target.** She stands on the side away from what she points at, and swaps sides as needed.
5. **Explained at the moment of use.** Heroic Summon is explained as you summon, the rows as you deploy, energy as you fight. Nothing is explained up front.
6. **Callouts point at things.** A boxed line with an arrow points at the exact element, and two can show at once.
7. **Reward early and often.** The free pull, the ten-pull, level-ups, loot and Victory all come within minutes.
8. **Conveniences are taught.** Skip cutscene, battle speed, Auto-equip, Get, and the back arrow.
9. **Navigation is taught.** The glove points at the back arrow every time, so the town-to-screen-and-back loop becomes second nature.
10. **What's next is visible.** Bubbles over locked stages show what clearing them unlocks.
11. **The guide stays reachable.** She tells you where to find her again.
12. **The first battles are short and sure.** They are quick wins, with generous loot.

## 3. Their screen design

- **The town is the hub.**
  - A wide scene you scroll sideways. Every building has a **name plaque**, and a red dot marks one with something waiting.
  - The player's portrait and level sit top left, and currencies (icon, number, +) top centre. Event icons sit top right, and social icons in a column on the left.
  - The main buttons (Quests, Guild, Bag, Hero, menu) run along the bottom right, beside one big round button.
- **Every screen has the same frame.** A back arrow top left, currencies top centre, and round **info (i)** and **help (?)** buttons top right. You always know how to leave and where to ask.
- **Popups look alike.** A framed panel with its title on a tab at the top, and a round red ✕ at its top-right corner.
- **Buttons are colour-coded** and say one or two words:
  - yellow for most actions (OK, Confirm, Summon)
  - green for gaining things (Get, Auto-equip, level up)
  - orange for the main action (Battle)
  - red-orange for undoing (Unequip)
- **Icons with numbers, everywhere.** Costs, rewards and currencies are never sentences.
- **Hero portraits carry their facts.** Stars (rarity) along the bottom, faction top left, and level top right. You read a hero without words.
- **Big art where it matters.**
  - the summon reveal
  - the hero on a pedestal on their screen
  - the battle sprites
  - the guide herself
- **A lean battle screen.** It has a menu button, the stage and round number, the speed button and the team auras, and nothing else.
- **Victory is a banner, a row of rewards and one button.** The battle's statistics sit behind a small button.
- **Readable type.** A bold, rounded face with a dark outline on light panels, and every title on a banner.
- **Few things per screen,** with generous margins and the panels centred.

**What stays our own:**
- the pixel art and the cozy-gothic palette
- the fire and the camp
- turn flags and rules, our strategy layer, which Idle Heroes has no equivalent of
- fatigue and Renown

We take their patterns, which are common across the genre. We don't take their art, characters or names.

**Translated into our look:**
- Their light parchment panels become our dark panels with amber trim and title tabs.
- Their outlined font becomes our three: Pirata One for titles, Silkscreen for labels, Spectral for lines.
- Their glove becomes a pixel-art pointer.
- Their guide's painted portrait becomes a large pixel-art Wick.

## 5. Decisions taken from this study

These answer the four questions on the camp mockups.
1. **The camp is layout A,** scrolling sideways like their town, with a name plaque under every building. The first six plots fit on one screen.
2. **Nothing else is missing from the mockups,** apart from the plaques and Wick's help corner (5.4).
3. **Set out opens the Road with the next stage's card already open:** two taps to a battle, like their Campaign → Battle. The camp's Road button opens the map with no card.
4. **Plots open from the middle outwards,** so a small camp needs no scrolling.
5. **Wick asks the player's name** for the company, shown with Renown. *(Optional: say if you'd rather skip it.)*

## 6. Screen by screen

The order follows the path a new player walks. For each screen:
1. A quick mockup in our style, unless the step is small.
2. You approve it.
3. A builder session builds it, ending in a PR.
4. You play it.
5. Your notes go in `docs/playtest-log.md`.
6. The fixes are built, then on to the next screen.
