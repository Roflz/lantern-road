Build step 2 · Rules

Rules editor

Each hero's list of conditions and actions, plus the party rules and saved rule sets. The rule cards follow the game's own: the condition is written as a sentence, and the action is shown as a skill with its icon and mana cost.

Flow and interaction

  • Rules are the fourth tab of each hero's sheet, so you tune one hero's gear, skills and rules in one place. The ‹ › arrows (or a swipe) step to the next hero and stay on the Rules tab.
  • Press and hold the grip to lift a row, then drag it. A plain swipe still scrolls. The numbers recount as the row moves.
  • The ON/OFF box turns a rule off without deleting it, the same as in the step 2 editor.
  • A rule that can't fire says why in red, for example when its skill isn't equipped.
  • “Fired 14×” comes from the last battle report, which ties the editor to what you watched.
  • Adding a rule is one sheet in two parts: pick the condition, then the action. Actions that can't follow the condition are greyed with the reason. Locked conditions say where to earn them, and new ones carry an ember dot.
  • A hint warns when the order makes a rule unreachable. It never blocks you.

Open questions

  1. Should Tactics also get its own bottom-bar tab that lists all six heroes' rules on one scroll?
  2. Do saved sets hold only rules, or full loadouts with formation and equipped skills too?
  3. Should “Fired ×” count the last battle or the whole push?
  4. Can you edit rules between battles mid-push, or only at the Hearth?
✦ RevisedBuild step 3 · Characters

Character sheet

The hero now stands in full at the top of the sheet, as they look in battle. Tap a skill under the stage, or tap the hero, to watch it play out on a training dummy. The stats fit in one compact grid. XP and fatigue sit on the stage itself.

Flow and interaction

  • The top of the sheet is a small stage. It shows the hero's full battle sprite at 7× scale, lit by a lantern, next to a training dummy.
  • Tap a Watch button, or tap the hero, to play that skill with the same effects as in battle: the projectile, the hit flash, healing motes and floating numbers. This mockup plays each one when you tap it.
  • XP runs along the bottom edge of the stage. Fatigue sits in the top corner with its Weary and Exhausted marks. Both stay visible without taking a row each.
  • The seven stats fit in a 4 × 2 grid with icons. The eighth tile is the next rule slot, so all three ways to spend points sit together. The skill tree lives on the Skills tab.
  • While you have points, each tile shows a +. Points stay pending until Confirm.
  • In the skill tree, the selected node's card plays its skill on a small stage, so you see what you're buying.
  • Swapping the equipped skill names the rules that stop working.

Open questions

  1. Should the dummy be a fixed training dummy, or a foe from the last battle?
  2. Does a point give a fixed amount per stat, or follow each class's growth?
  3. Can you refund a single skill rank, or only respec the whole tree?

Decided

  • The full stage shows on the Stats and Gear tabs, as drawn. Skills and Rules keep the room for their content.
✦ RevisedBuild step 4 · Equipment

Equipment

Gear shows on the sprite. Each weapon, each armour weight and some accessories draw differently, so you can read a hero's kit at a glance in camp and in battle. Tap the weapon, armour or accessory tiles in frame A to try them on.

Flow and interaction

  • The Gear tab keeps the stage. Tapping an item in the bag puts it on the sprite at once, so you see it before you equip it. The slot it would fill lights up, and the stage says “Trying on”.
  • Gear changes how the sprite is drawn:
    • Each weapon has its own shape: a staff, a swinging censer that trails smoke, or a crook.
    • Each armour weight draws differently: a robe for light, chain for medium and plate with pauldrons for heavy.
    • Some accessories show, such as the rosary beads or a reliquary that glows.
    The battle screen uses the same layered sprite.
  • The item card opens with a Now and With pair of sprites, then three bands in a fixed order: Always, Costs and When. Each condition is checked against where the hero stands now, so a front-row bonus on a back-row hero reads “Off”.
  • An item the hero can't use shows the hero who can, with and without it, and offers to equip it on them.
  • Rarity is shown by both colour and a word. Every stat line carries its icon.

Open questions

  1. Sell for gold, salvage for materials, or both?
  2. What's the bag limit, and what happens to loot found mid-push when the bag is full?
  3. Do we want an “Equip best” button, or does it undercut the trade-offs?
  4. With every item drawn, should items share a few base shapes per weapon type and armour weight, recoloured and trimmed per item, to keep the art manageable?

Decided

  • Every item changes the sprite: every weapon, every armour and every accessory.
Build steps 3 and 5 · Party

Formation and party

Six places, 2 in front and 4 behind, filled from the roster tray. The board is drawn top-down with the foes at the top, so the back row sits nearest your thumb. Tokens show each hero's full sprite in their current gear.

Flow and interaction

  • The Party tab opens on this board. Foes are at the top, so the two front places are nearest the enemy and the four back places sit near your thumb.
  • Drag a hero from the tray onto a place. Dropping on a filled place swaps the two heroes. Tapping a token opens the hero sheet.
  • While you drag, the places that suit that hero glow, and the board warns about placements the battle rules punish. It warns but never blocks.
  • Tokens show the full sprite in its current gear, a fatigue sliver that turns red at Weary, and the level.
  • Resting and Spent heroes stay in the tray, dimmed, with the time until they're ready.

Open questions

  1. If the front row falls, does the back row step forward, or do melee foes then reach the back row?
  2. Should formations be saved separately, or as part of a rule set?
✦ RevisedBuild step 5 · Home base

The Hearth

The home base is a camp. Your party sits around the fire in the middle, with building plots in a ring around them. Drag sideways in frame A to walk around the fire, or tap a hero to hear from them. You choose what to build on each plot. Renown, your account level, clears more plots as you progress through the game. Plots you haven't unlocked yet are simply gaps in the ring.

Flow and interaction

  • The six heroes in the current party sit on logs around the fire, drawn with the same sprites and gear as in battle. Each has a small habit: Brannoc hones his blade, Isolde's hands give off motes, Maud's censer smokes, Wick's lamp breathes and Ottilie's shield catches the light. Tap one and they look up, say a line, and their card offers their sheet.
  • Drag sideways anywhere to turn the camp around the fire. On release it eases to the nearest building, which faces you and gets an ember outline. The ‹ › buttons and the arrow keys step one building at a time, so it works one-handed and without dragging.
  • The ring is made of building plots, plus the road out of camp, and it's the only way into buildings. You start with 3 plots and choose what to build on each. The ring holds one plot per building type, which is the most you can have. Until then, every plot is a choice. Plots you haven't unlocked are gaps in the ring, and stepping skips them.
  • A newly cleared plot shows as a staked-out patch with a sign and a + badge. Its card offers Build, which opens a sheet of the buildings you can build there, with what each does and its cost. Barracks and Shrine are on offer in frame B because this save hasn't built them.
  • Any building can be torn down from its own screen (frame F) to free its plot. Nothing comes back, so a building you raise again starts from scratch.
  • Renown is the account level, separate from hero levels. It grows from overall progress: new stages lit, bosses beaten, hero levels and buildings built. For now it only clears plots. More recruit picks and a bigger roster still come from the Recruit Board and Barracks. Renown sits as a lantern badge in the corner of the camp and opens its own track.
  • The front building's card at the bottom shows its status and main action (Open, Collect, Build or Set out). Buildings that want you show a badge from anywhere in the ring.
  • “While you were away” opens once over the camp when you return. It lists gains per building, celebrates level-ups and flags storage that filled up.

Open questions

None left for the Hearth.

Decided

  • The account level is called Renown. It rises with overall progress, and for now it only unlocks plots.
  • You start with 3 plots, to be tuned after play-testing. The most you can have is one per building type.
  • Locked plots are gaps in the ring until they unlock.
  • You can tear a building down to free its plot, and you get nothing back.
  • Recruit picks and roster size come from buildings.
  • The ring is the only way into buildings. There is no list view.
  • The recruit board shows its refresh timer (frame D).
  • Heroes at the fire have idle animations and react when tapped.
Build step 6 · Campaign

The Lantern Road

Each region is one tall map read from the bottom up. Cleared stages are lit lanterns and the road ahead is dark. Tapping a stage opens the sheet that sends the party out.

Flow and interaction

  • Each region map reads from the bottom up, so the frontier stage sits near your thumb and the dark lies above. Lit stages throw pools of light, so the map shows how far your light reaches.
  • Red diamonds are elite stages and the skull at the top is the region's boss. The party's marker pulses on the frontier stage.
  • Tapping a stage opens its sheet, which shows the foes as their battle sprites, the rewards and the push settings. Set out is the last button, at thumb height.
  • A cleared stage shows the same sheet with Farm instead of Set out. The party strip flags tired heroes, and Edit jumps to the Party board.
  • The region title opens the whole road. Regions you haven't reached stay in darkness.

Open questions

  1. Should a push carry on into the next region by itself after the boss falls?
  2. Are elite stages on the main road, as drawn, or on side branches you can skip?
  3. Do “Come home when” settings belong on the stage sheet, in party rules, or both?
