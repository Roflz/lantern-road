# Story Options

From play-test note 2.3 (2026-11-04): the lantern-lighting premise "got old a long time ago and is frankly really stupid at this point". It worked as something to build on, and now the game needs a real plot.

**Chosen:** `docs/story.md`, spine A, "the Fallen Kingdom" (2026-11-05). This file keeps the earlier options and why they were dropped.

## Decided so far (2026-11-04)

- **Tone:** more dry humour, and lighter and more approachable in general. Spooky but cosy, and never grim for long.
- **The game can be renamed.**
- **Rejected (note 2.4):** Option 1 made lighter, near the end: "makes absolutely no sense, it is complete nonsense words".
  - It invented rules to explain every mechanic, then stacked jokes on top. Its basic questions had no answers: how do five people kill Death, and why would that stop all dying?
  - Next time: first a story that makes sense, told plainly. The humour goes in how the characters talk, not in the world's rules.

## What the story has to do

- **Explain the strange rules.** Heroes who fall get back up when the battle ends, and the road is full of the dead. A good story makes these make sense.
- **Give the five bosses one plot.** They're already named: the Sexton, the Drowned Bishop, the Antlered One, the Burnt Marshal and the Hollow King.
- **Fit in one sentence.** A new player can say who they are and what they're after.
- **Give the guide a real part,** not just a voice.
- **Feed the systems:** income while you're away, recruits, the base, loot.
- **Keep the look:** a warm fire in a cold, dark, gothic world.
- **Be fresh.** No "restore the lights along the road", and no "collect the five pieces".

**What stays, whichever we pick:**
- the battles, tactics, gear, skills, fatigue and formation
- the five regions and their bosses
- the classes
- the camp and its buildings, though some get new names
- the road view, with stages along a road

**What goes:** lanterns as the point of the game. The title "Lantern Road" fits none of these four.

## Option 1: The Five Who Killed Death

**Hook:** "Five people killed Death. Now nothing dies, and Death's ghost wants them found."

**The story:**
- Death used to walk the land and gather the dying, gently. Some years ago, five people met it at a crossroads and killed it. Each of them had a reason.
- Since then, nothing dies. The old lie in their beds and can't finish. The buried claw their way back up, rotting and confused. The land fills with the dead who can't stop.
- Death didn't quite go. Its ghost lingers at an old fire on the edge of the Gravefields, thin as smoke.
- That's where you come in: a captain with a company to raise. The ghost has five names. Find them, and make them die.

**You:** the captain of a company of heroes, working for a ghost.

**The guide:** Death's ghost, gentle, tired and dry. It could keep the name Wick. In the old tale of Godfather Death, Death keeps a candle burning for every life.

**The five, and why they did it:**
1. **The Sexton** buried too many children, and wanted never to dig another grave. Now he digs the dead back up.
2. **The Drowned Bishop** prayed for his flock to survive the flood. They did: drowned, and still singing under the water.
3. **The Antlered One,** an old god of the wood, was dying as people forgot it. Killing Death was how it stayed. Now its wood grows and grows, and nothing rots.
4. **The Burnt Marshal** wanted his soldiers never to fall. They burned with his keep, and they're burning still, holding it for no one.
5. **The Hollow King** brought the five together. He wears the crown he took from Death, and the dead kneel to it.

**The ending:** when the last of them dies, Death comes back. The old can finish, and the dead lie down. Your heroes can die again. It's bittersweet, and Death has more work for a company like yours.

**How the systems fit:**
- **Heroes get back up** because nobody can die. "The one good thing about all this," says the ghost.
- **Fatigue:** they can't die, but they can wear out. They rest at the fire.
- **Each stage you win** lays the dead there to rest.
- **Idle income:** where the dead lie down again, the living come back, and someone has to bury the backlog. The grieving pay your company for burials while you're away. "Business is good. People are dying again."
- **Recruits:** the grieving, the tired, and sellswords who need the pay.
- **Gear** is grave goods the dead carried out of their graves. **Relics** are each killer's treasure.

**Tone:** gothic and sad, with the ghost's dry humour.

**Watch out:** "a land where nothing dies" is the starting point of Death's Door (2021) and of Pratchett's *Reaper Man*. The murder, the five killers with their reasons, and the ghost are ours.

**The first minute, as four painted panels:**
1. A hooded figure in a field at dusk, carrying a candle: "Everything dies. That was my work, and I was gentle with it."
2. Five figures at a crossroads, the hooded one fallen: "Then five people met me at a crossroads, and killed me."
3. Graves opening, and an old woman in bed with her eyes open: "Now nothing dies. The old can't finish. The buried won't stay down."
4. The ghost at the fire, looking up at you: "I have their names. I need someone to make them die."

Then the welcome: "You'll want heroes. Good news: right now, they can't die. Nobody can."

## Option 2: The Heir

**Hook:** "The dead have picked their next king. It's you. They just need you dead first."

**The story:**
- The Hollow King ruled the dead for three hundred years. Last winter his throne stood empty, and his crown went looking for an heir. It picked you, a nobody with no idea why.
- The dead can only crown one of their own. So every dead thing between here and the Hollow Throne is coming to kill you, very politely, so you can be crowned.
- An old man named Wick, who knows far more than he says, hides you at a walled camp on the edge of the Gravefields. He helps you hire a company to stand between you and the dead.
- The dead won't stop while their court stands, so you take the fight up the road to them, lord by lord.
- On the way, you learn why the crown chose you, and what Wick did the night you were born.

**You:** the unwilling heir.

**The guide:** Wick, with a secret.

**The five, and what each wants with you:**
1. **The Sexton,** the court's gravedigger, has dug your grave already.
2. **The Drowned Bishop** would anoint you the old way: by drowning.
3. **The Antlered One,** an old god, was king of the dead before the Hollow King, and wants the crown back.
4. **The Burnt Marshal,** the court's general, is sworn to deliver the heir. He's honourable, and he doesn't stop.
5. **The Hollow King** isn't gone after all. The crown won't let its wearer die until it has an heir, and he wants out.

**The ending:** break the crown, and the dead rest at last. Or wear it alive, as a living king of the dead. That ending suits an endgame of ruling them.

**How the systems fit:**
- **Heroes get back up** because of the court's oldest law: none may take the heir's sworn.
- **The endless dead on the road** are coming for you.
- **Idle income is tribute.** Half the dead already think of you as their king. As each lord falls, his vassals send gold and grave goods, even while you're away.
- **Recruits:** sellswords and the desperate, paid to guard you.
- **Tactics:** they protect you; you don't fight, you command.

**Tone:** gothic and wry (polite assassins who bring gifts), with a mystery about your past.

**Watch out:** it's a chosen-one story, but turned around: being chosen is the threat.

## Option 3: The Tithe

**Hook:** "Every autumn your village gives the dead one soul. This year it's your sister, and you said no."

**The story:**
- Long ago the village's founders made a bargain with the Hollow King. No one in the village would ever die. In return, every autumn, the dead would take one villager up the road.
- For a hundred years it held. This year the bell named your sister, and you barred the gate.
- The bargain is broken, and the dead are coming to collect everything. Wick, the village's old bell-ringer, helps you raise a company from anyone who will fight.
- The only way to end it is to go up the road to the one who holds the bargain.
- On the way, you learn:
  - what the founders really traded
  - where the tithed souls went (some are among the foes you fight, and some can be brought home)
  - who in the village has been helping the dead choose

**You:** the one who said no.

**The guide:** Wick, the bell-ringer, who has rung the tithe bell for fifty years.

**The five:**
1. **The Sexton** is the village's own gravedigger, who keeps the tithe-book.
2. **The Drowned Bishop's** chapel blessed the bargain.
3. **The Antlered One** is the god the founders prayed to first, before they turned to the King.
4. **The Burnt Marshal** tried to break the bargain once, and burned for it.
5. **The Hollow King** holds the bargain.

**The ending:** you break the bargain for good and bring the tithed home, your sister among them. The village can die again, and it's free.

**How the systems fit:**
- **Heroes get back up** because the bargain still protects the village's people. Breaking it at the end ends that too.
- **The base** is the village itself, walled and rebuilt. Its buildings are the village's.
- **Idle income:** the village's harvest and market while you're away.
- **Recruits:** villagers and passing sellswords.

**Tone:** folk horror, a cosy village with a dreadful secret.

**Watch out:** it's the darkest of the four. The camp becomes a village, which suits its buildings but changes how the Hearth is described.

## Option 4: The Last Inn

**Hook:** "You died. The road to rest is blocked, and someone has to keep the inn."

**The story:**
- When people die, they walk the Long Road to the Gate at its end, and rest. Inns along the road gave them a fire for the night.
- The Hollow King refused to go through. He took the throne before the Gate and shut it.
- Now the dead pile up on the road. The longer they wait, the more they go hollow. The monsters you fight were travellers once.
- You died (you don't remember how) and reached the last inn with its fire still lit. Its keeper, Wick, is fading, and he gives you the keys.
- A keeper can't walk the road. So you take in the dead who still have fight in them, and send them out to clear the way.

**You:** the new keeper.

**The guide:** Wick, the old keeper, fading.

**The five:**
1. **The Sexton** buries new arrivals so they can't walk on.
2. **The Drowned Bishop** holds the river crossing and keeps the drowned.
3. **The Antlered One** is the old wood where the dead shed their memories, gone wild.
4. **The Burnt Marshal** holds the King's approach, with a garrison that burns forever.
5. **The Hollow King** sits at the Gate.

**The ending:** you open the Gate, and everyone can pass, your heroes too. Do you go through, or keep the inn?

**How the systems fit:**
- **Heroes get back up** because they're dead already. They come back together by the inn's fire.
- **Fatigue:** the dead fade, and the fire restores them.
- **Gear** is grave goods, rarer for grander burials.
- **Idle income is obols,** the coins left with the dead. Every soul you free stops at your inn and pays one.
- **Recruits:** new arrivals who choose to fight. The Recruit Board is the inn's guest book.
- **Tactics:** a keeper can't leave the inn, so you give your guests standing orders.

**Tone:** melancholy and cosy, the warmest of the four.

**Watch out:** the afterlife is well-used ground (Grim Fandango, Spiritfarer, Hades), and "you died" is a downbeat start.

## My pick

**Option 1, The Five Who Killed Death.** It explains the most with the least strain:
- why your heroes get back up
- why the dead walk
- why each boss is a person with a reason, worth learning about
- a guide with real character, who can stay Wick

Each region has a name to hunt and a story to uncover, and the ending has weight. Option 2 is the freshest if you'd rather the story were about the player.

## Option 1, made lighter

It's the same story, told with a straight face about absurd things:
- The dead are more stubborn than scary.
- Each villain got exactly what they wished for, and it went wrong.
- Death's ghost is the calmest one in the room.

**The premise:**
- Death kept a candle for every life. When one burned down, Death came round, politely, to collect.
- Then five people killed Death. Each had a wish.
- Now nobody dies. Grandad is 140 and furious about it. The graveyards empty every night, and supper won't stay on the plate.
- What's left of Death is a small hooded ghost with a candle flame for a face. He calls himself the late Death. Everyone calls him Wick.
- Wick keeps a ledger of everyone who's overdue, and five names sit at the top. He put up a notice for help, and you answered it.

**You:** you run the company. You hire the heroes, send them down the road and keep the books, because Wick can't lift anything.

**Wick's voice:** calm, polite, a little bureaucratic, endlessly patient. For example:
- "I'm Death. The late Death, technically."
- "Nobody dies any more. Terrible for the world. Wonderful for your heroes."
- "They can't die. They can get very tired, though. Let them sit by the fire."
- "Overdue by eleven years. Some people have no manners."

**The five, and how their wishes went:**
1. **The Sexton** wanted to put his spade down for good. Now the dead dig themselves up every night, and he's never been busier.
2. **The Drowned Bishop** wanted a congregation that would never leave. Then the river came up. They're still in the pews, singing under the water. Attendance has never been better.
3. **The Antlered One,** an old god of the wood, didn't want to be forgotten. Now nothing in its wood rots, autumn never ends, and the leaves are forty feet deep. Nobody's forgotten it. Nobody can get out of it.
4. **The Burnt Marshal** wanted his soldiers never to fall. They caught fire holding the keep six years ago, and they're still on duty. Morale is low.
5. **The Hollow King** wanted to live forever, and he does. His court has had his funeral planned for sixty years. The cake is very stale.

**The ending:** when the last of them dies, Wick is Death again, tall and whole. Your heroes can die again, someday. "Not for a long while," he says. "I'll be gentle. I always was." The company stays in business, because there's a lot of overdue paperwork.

**How the systems fit:**
- **Heroes get back up** because nobody can die.
- **Fatigue:** they can't die, but they can get very tired.
- **The dead on the road** are the restless: they got up and won't lie back down. Beat them, and they lie down looking relieved.
- **Each stage you win** lays a stretch of the road to rest.
- **Income while you're away:** families pay the company to lay their restless relatives to rest. Some pay extra to keep Grandad until Sunday dinner. The Toll House becomes the Undertaker's.
- **The Recruit Board:** "Help wanted. Must not mind the dead. Dying not included."
- **Gear:** the restless took their grave goods with them when they got up. Loot is getting it back.
- **The Hearth stays.** It's the company's camp around the old fire, where Wick sits.
- **The Old Beacon** on the hill above becomes Death's house. It has been dark since he died, and lights again at the end.

**The first minute,** four painted panels told by Wick:
1. Death, tall and hooded, in a room full of candles: "I kept a candle for every life. When one burned down, I came round. Politely."
2. Five figures at a crossroads, and Death on the ground, very surprised: "Then five people killed me. Rude."
3. A skeleton climbing out of its grave, an old man shaking his stick, and a roast chicken walking off a plate: "Now nobody dies. The graveyards empty every night, and supper won't stay on the plate."
4. Wick at the fire, small, with a candle flame for a face and a ledger under his arm: "I'm what's left. Call me Wick. And I have five names that are very overdue."

**Then the welcome:**
- "You answered the notice. Good. Nobody else did."
- "What do they call you?"
- "[Name]. Welcome to the company. It's yours. I can't lift anything."
- On the Recruit Board: "You'll need heroes. Good news: right now, none of them can die."
- On Set out: "The restless are just down the road. Go and lay them to rest."
- After the first win: "See? They lie down, they feel better. Everyone wins."

## Names for the game

| Name | Why |
|---|---|
| **Grim & Co.** | A company doing Death's work while Death is out. It says what you do, with a straight face. |
| **Rest Assured** | Laying the restless to rest, said reassuringly. The warmest of the six. |
| **The Late Death** | Wick's own joke about himself. |
| **Overdue** | Wick's ledger. Short and dry. |
| **Unfinished Business** | Ghosts have it, and nobody can finish. |
| **Nobody Dies** | The premise, plainly. |

**My pick:** Grim & Co., with Rest Assured close behind. We'd check the app stores for clashes before deciding.
